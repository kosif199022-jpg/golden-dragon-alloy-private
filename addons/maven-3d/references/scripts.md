# Script library: the "In this world…" formula, the 8 analysed stories, hooks, templates (English and Arabic)

The analysed video was a 19-minute compilation of eight 9:16 stories. The beat sheets below are summaries of how each story is built. They are not transcripts, so use them as structure, never as text to copy. Write original premises.

## 1. The formula
| beat | job | share of runtime | example line shape |
|---|---|---|---|
| RULE | an impossible rule, stated as plain fact | first 3–5 s | "In this world, every X does Y." / «في هذا العالم، كلُّ … » |
| EXAMPLES | 2–3 quick proofs of the rule, one shot each | 5–15% | "One year it was A. Another year, B." |
| STAKES | the cost or deadline of the rule | 5% | "But if you're still inside when it closes, you're gone forever." |
| HOOK | the hero, plus a personal want or wound | 10% | "Five years ago, Ryan's dad went in and never came back." |
| INCITING | "Then, one morning…" | 5% | «وفي صباحِ أحد الأيام…» |
| ESCALATION | discoveries and set pieces, each bigger than the last | 40% | short sentences, every noun a new picture |
| WARNING/TWIST | a line that flips what we believed | 5% | "We don't have a dog." / "Don't trust the sky." |
| CLIMAX | a countdown or chase | 15% | "20 seconds!" "10 seconds!" "5 seconds!" |
| PAYOFF | a callback to the hook, or a twist sting | last 3–5 s | "Happy birthday, Ryan." / "Wait… he paused both of them?" |

A 10 s Short keeps only RULE, one stake or example, HOOK and TWIST/PAYOFF.

**Style rules**
- Short declarative sentences, mostly under 10 words.
- Big simple words: enormous, gigantic, thousands, suddenly, never, forever.
- Present the rule as normal, never as a question.
- Use first person ("In my town…") for horror and comedy, and third person for adventure.
- Dialogue is one to three words in quotes ("Dad!", "Run!", "It's here!"), voiced by the narrator.
- Use numbers ("60 minutes", "47 seconds", "40 years"), because they become props: timers and signs.

**Word budgets at Maven pace**

| length | English (~4.0 words/s) | Arabic (~2.4 words/s) |
|---|---|---|
| 10 s | 36–40 words | 22–25 words |
| 30 s | ~115 words | ~70 words |
| 60 s | ~230 words | ~140 words |
| 2:30 (a full ref story) | ~570 words | ~350 words |

## 2. The 8 analysed stories (beat sheets)
1. **The mystery cube** (adventure, 3rd person, 2:52).
   - **Rule:** once a year a giant black cube covered in gold glyphs lands in every town. Each holds a portal to another world, and it stays open for one hour. Anyone left inside is never seen again.
   - **Examples:** dinosaurs, a gold city, an ocean.
   - **Hook:** the hero's father vanished inside one five years ago after promising him a birthday gift.
   - **Inciting:** a new cube lands.
   - **Escalation:** an ice world, then the same statue from the father's world, a hatch, a tunnel of themed doors, the father's door, an empty paradise.
   - **Twist:** the father is chained up and warns "don't trust the sky". A beast with thousands of eyes appears.
   - **Climax:** an escape against the countdown (20 / 10 / 5 seconds), with the doors slamming shut.
   - **Payoff:** the father pulls out the promised gift. "Happy birthday."
2. **Hide-and-seek with a tall stranger** (horror, 1st person).
   - **Rule:** once a year the whole town hides from a thin figure with a ticking clock for a heart, who counts in the square. Whoever he finds is gone.
   - **Escalation:** the rules get explained; the ticking speeds up as he gets closer.
   - **Twist:** the narrator realises they are the one who found the hidden friend ("I found you").
3. **The moon at the window** (horror, 1st person).
   - **Rule:** on full-moon nights the moon comes down and looks in through windows. Families board them up.
   - **Escalation:** a note says "leave a crack". Mum's bandaged fingers, scratches inside the door, "it was the dog".
   - **Twist:** "We don't have a dog." Mum's voice knocks at the door, humming wrong.
4. **The parrot emperor** (comedy, 1st person).
   - **Rule:** the empire is ruled through a golden parrot that repeats the emperor's orders.
   - **Reveal:** the emperor died years ago, and the parrot just keeps repeating.
   - **Escalation:** the first ever vote. Payoff: the parrot wins by a landslide.
5. **The guide voice** (horror twist, 1st person).
   - **Rule:** everyone has an inner voice, a guide that is never wrong.
   - **Hook:** a kind guide that tells jokes.
   - **Twist:** the voice was something pretending, waiting to be trusted. "You did so good."
6. **Married couples swap ages, and the colour kingdom** (two short pieces).
   - **Ages:** a rich old man marries a poor young girl to become young; the twist is on him.
   - **Colours:** three colour tribes must never mix. Then a purple child is born, the king's anger turns to change, and "colours nobody had seen before".
7. **The pause remote** (40 s twist).
   - **Rule:** one random person a day gets a remote that freezes anyone until they are unpaused.
   - **Story:** a bullied kid pauses the bully, then smashes the remote so no one can undo it.
   - **Twist:** he paused his best friend too.
8. **The sky colour** (adventure, 3rd person).
   - **Rule:** every morning the sky's colour decides the day's power. Green, everyone flies; yellow, everything you touch turns to gold.
   - **Escalation:** a colour nobody has seen, a tower, chains.
   - **Payoff:** a rainbow sky, so everyone chooses their own power.

**What repeats:**
- The rule is introduced in the first 5 words.
- A personal loss or want sits under the spectacle.
- A short dialogue line lands at the turn.
- The ending recontextualises the rule. Either the rule's cost lands on the hero, or the hero breaks the rule's logic.

## 4. Templates
**10 s** (rule, twist; about 38 English words / 23 Arabic words):
> In this world, [RULE]. But [COST OR CATCH]. [HERO] [DID THE THING]. [TIME JUMP], [SHOCKING RESULT].

**30 s** (rule, 2 examples, hook, inciting moment, twist):
> In this world, [RULE]. [EXAMPLE 1]. [EXAMPLE 2]. But [STAKES]. [HERO] had always [WANT/WOUND]. Then one morning, [INCITING]. [ESCALATION ×2]. Suddenly, [TWIST LINE]. "[1–3 WORD LINE]"

**60–180 s**: the full formula in section 1. Put a countdown in the climax and call back the hook at the end.

## 5. Arabic notes
- Write in simple fusha (فصحى), using short sentences with the verb first.
- Use diacritics only where they prevent a misreading. The aligner ignores them, so they never break timing.
- Write numbers as words in the script so the voice reads them correctly (عشر ثوانٍ, not ١٠ ثوانٍ). Signs and props can show digits.
- Voices (Edge TTS, free):
  - `ar-SA-HamedNeural`: a clear fusha narrator, the default.
  - `ar-IQ-BasselNeural`: Iraqi accent.
  - `ar-EG-ShakirNeural`: Egyptian.
  - `ar-SY-LaithNeural`: Levantine.
  - For a premium voice, put a recording at `vo/raw.wav` (for example ElevenLabs multilingual, after asking before spending credits) and set `"engine": "file"`.
- Captions join a short word (في، من، يا، و) to the next word and run right-to-left in Lalezar automatically.

## 6. The two worked examples (in `examples/`)
- **dragons-en (10 s):**
  > In this world, every kid gets one wish on their tenth birthday. But whatever you wish for, the whole town gets it too. Leo wished for a dragon. The next morning, the sky went dark. Thousands of dragons.
- **door-ar (13 s):**
  > في هذا العالم، كلُّ بابٍ في المدرسة يفتحُ على عالمٍ مختلف. في الصباح، فتح سامي بابَ الصف، فوجد نفسه في قلعةٍ قديمة. نظر إليه الملك وقال: أخيرًا وصلت.
