## The script formula (the same in every story)
1. **Rule line first, word one:** "In this world, …" / "In this town, …" / "In my town, …". It states an impossible rule as plain fact.
2. **Two or three quick examples of the rule** ("One year a box revealed a jungle filled with dinosaurs. Another contained a city made of gold…"). Each example gets its own shot.
3. **Stakes and a deadline:** "Every box stays open for exactly 1 hour… anyone still inside is never seen again."
4. **A personal hook:** a lost parent, a promise, a birthday ("He promised Ryan he'd bring him a piece for his birthday").
5. **Inciting moment:** "Then, one morning…"
6. **Discovery and escalation** through set pieces, with a short dialogue line in quotes ("Dad!", "Run!").
7. **A warning or twist line:** "Don't trust the sky." / "We don't have a dog."
8. **Countdown climax:** "20 seconds!" "10 seconds!" "5 seconds!"
9. **Payoff that calls back the hook** ("Happy birthday, Ryan."), or a twist sting for the short ones ("Wait… he had accidentally paused both of them?").

Language: short declarative sentences, simple words, lots of "enormous / gigantic / thousands / suddenly". Narration is fast: **3.75–4.4 words a second** (measured from captions, about 245 a minute), with no pauses between sentences.

## Pace and camera (measured on the first 2 minutes)
- **A new shot about every 1.1–1.3 s** (scene-change detector: 102 shots in 120 s at the sensitive setting, median 1.12 s; whole video median 1.23 s). Nearly every cut lands on the noun being spoken: "black" = the cube in the sky, "statue" = the statue, "Dad!" = a close-up of the face.
- **The camera never stops.** It uses push-ins, orbits, low-angle tilts up, FPV fly-throughs (0:00 flying over the town, 1:16 down the tunnel) and handheld drift.
- **Shots are joined by whip-pans and motion blur**, not hard cuts. The frame before and after a cut is smeared, as in the 0:01 and 0:17 sheets.
- **Shot vocabulary:**
  - an establishing fly-over;
  - a low angle up at something huge (the cube, the statue, the creature);
  - a medium shot of the hero walking away from camera;
  - a **face punch-in** on the reaction (fills the frame, about 1 s);
  - an insert of an object (the countdown timer, door symbols, a wrist timer);
  - a crowd wide shot.
- **Reaction faces are the emotional beats**: wide white eyes with tiny pupils, a gaping trapezoid mouth with teeth and tongue, at 1:07, 1:10, 1:26, 1:43 and 1:56.

## Picture look
- **Real 3D renders** from a game engine. Blocky avatars sit in game-built sets under "ultra" lighting:
  - strong bloom and glow;
  - heavy depth of field, so the background blurs behind close-ups;
  - saturated, punchy colour;
  - per-scene grades: warm gold for the cube and paradise, icy blue-purple for the frozen world, dark night for the creature.
- **Sets are large and built from simple blocks:**
  - the town square with tiled paving;
  - rows of pastel houses and round "broccoli" trees;
  - the giant black cube with glowing gold glyphs;
  - an ice field with crystal spikes;
  - a stone tunnel.
- Skies are bright blue with puffy clouds, or night purple with stars.
- Particles: falling leaves, snow, dust clouds, embers.

## Character shape
- **Classic blocky R6 proportions:**
  - The head is a rounded cube, about 1/4 of the total height.
  - The torso is 2 units wide by 2 high.
  - Arms and legs are each 1x2 blocks, as long as the torso is tall.
  - There are no hands or feet; the limbs end flat.
  - The limbs swing stiffly from the shoulder and hip.
- **The face is a flat cartoon decal on the front of the head:**
  - big white oval eyes with thick black outlines and small dot pupils;
  - thick angled eyebrows;
  - one black open mouth. Shock is a trapezoid with a white tooth bar and a tongue; joy is a wide D-shape; sad is closed eyes with arched lids.
  - Expressions swap instantly, with no in-betweening.
- **3D accessories** carry the identity: a swoopy brown hair mesh, a big grey beard on the father, a red puffer vest over a navy hoodie, a backpack with a bedroll, a wrist timer.
- Clothing is textured onto the blocks: quilted puffer lines, straps, pockets.
- Skin is the classic pale yellow-beige or a light tone. The cast is mostly 2–3 named characters plus crowds of tiny avatars.

## Captions
- **One word at a time** (sometimes 2–3 short words: "him a", "containing a"). Each word is timed exactly to the voice.
- Bold rounded geometric sans (Poppins ExtraBold / Bold look), lowercase as spoken, with punctuation kept ("then,", "close,", "Dad!" in quotes).
- **Pale-yellow fill (#fff59d-ish) with a thick black outline**, small (cap height about 2.3% of frame height), centred at **about 68% down the frame**.
- There is no box and no animation beyond the swap. Emphasis words are sometimes in caps ("GOLDEN trees").
- The 12:21 creator differs: two or three words, white with one key word coloured green, blue or red.

## Sound (measured with earshot on 0:00–0:30, and with ebur128 on the whole video)
- **-13.8 LUFS integrated**, LRA 5.4 LU overall (2.0 LU on the first 30 s), true peak -0.9 dBTP. It is loud and flat, a compressed Shorts master.
- A **music bed runs under the whole story** (about 146 BPM, bright "confident and cool" pop, pizzicato and vocal chops) with no gaps.
- **Whip-pan whooshes** on the transitions, plus **pop and hit accents** on reveals (strong accents at 0:05, 0:17, 0:24, 20–25 dB over the bed).
- One fast male narrator carries everything. Character lines are read by the same narrator with a little acting.

## Maven pace (the house setting, slightly slower than the refs)
The refs run at 3.75–4.4 words a second, and the first demo ran at 4.25. Maven pace is about **4.0 words/s in English** (Kokoro at speed 1.18, pauses squeezed to 0.18 s) and about **2.4 words/s in Arabic** (Edge TTS at +6%). Arabic words are longer, so the syllable rate is about the same.
- A cut lands about every 0.9–1.3 s, and never later than 2 s.
- A shot that holds longer than 1.6 s must move strongly: a crane, an orbit or a push.
- The last shot holds about 1 s after the final word (`run(S, { tail: 1.0 })`).

## Checklist (what makes it read as this style)
1. The first word is the rule, and the picture at second 0 is moving: a fly-over or a low angle on something huge.
2. Every noun in the voice has its own picture: "statue" shows the statue, "door" shows the door.
3. A face punch-in lands on every emotional beat (shock, joy, fear), using a big-eyes expression.
4. Whip-pans join the shots (automatic: `run()` adds them, and `noWhip` turns them off for a shot).
5. Depth of field is on in every shot: shallow on faces, deep on wides.
6. Each scene has its own colour grade: gold and warm for wonder, blue-purple for danger, warm indoor light for home.
7. Captions are one word (or a word plus a short word) in pale yellow with a black outline, about 68% down the frame.
8. Sound: a whoosh on most cuts, a hit on reveals, the voice always on top, mastered at -14 LUFS.
9. End on a twist line or a callback, not on a fade.
