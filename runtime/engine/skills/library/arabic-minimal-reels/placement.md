# Placement: which line gets which scene, and when it returns to the face

Measured across 20 short videos: 1,347.7 s of footage, 39,139 frames, 691 shots. Each shot's scene type was assigned by looking at it. Each line's kind was assigned by reading the transcript.

**Caveat:** I coded line kinds knowing which scene they sat on, so this is not a blind test. Read the table as a strong pattern, not proof. "Setup lines never leave the face" is partly shaped by this.

## The rhythm

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Time on the face | 62.6% pooled; per video 44% to 80% |
| Average seconds per shot | 1.91 pooled (median 1.5, 10th-90th 0.27-3.9) |
| Average seconds per shot, per video | 1.25 to 4.13, mean 2.01 |
| Stay on the face before leaving | mean 4.43 s, median 3.6 s, 10th-90th 1.4-7.9 s |
| Stay away from the face | mean 2.97 s, median 2.33 s, 10th-90th 1.2-5.2 s, max 11.2 s |
| Returns to the face per minute | mean 8.1; per video 4.0 to 12.8 |
| Scenes in one trip away from the face | 1 in 72% of trips (124 of 172), 2 in 17%, 3 in 7%, 4 or more in 4% |
| First time off the face | mean 3.5 s, median 2.9 s, latest 9 s |
| Last scene | face in 17 of 20 |
| Speech | 3.62 words per second |

So: leave, show one thing, come back. It is face, graphic, face, graphic, not long graphic runs.

Plain-language rules from this:

1. **Talking to the viewer stays on the face.** Questions to him, "let me explain", "so what do you do", instructions. Never cover these.
2. **Can you film it? Then film it.** A line about checking your phone gets a phone.
3. **Feeling, then a dark clip.** Emotion words get a moody clip with the word large.
4. **Name a person: portrait or painting.** Held about 1 s. The caption is often just the name, in a block.
5. **Quote: flat canvas with marks,** or stay on the face if the delivery is the point.
6. **A number or list step: the same graphic returns** with the new item, between face shots. Side illustration, list canvas, or a chart that gains a point.
7. **Up or down over time: a graph.**
8. **Definition: mostly stay on the face,** and the key term becomes a caption. When a definition has parts, use object scenes (circles, formula canvas).

A useful note for planning: announce a type on the face, then show it when you describe it.

The "minimal graphic" feel comes from a few of them among many clean dark clips. If a reel has no B-roll budget, text-centric and object scenes are the fallback.

## Opening and ending

- Opening: 10 of 20 start on the hook title over the face. 1 starts on a title over a B-roll montage, 1 on a painting, 8 on a plain captioned face.
- Ending: 17 of 20 end on the face. Last shot is usually long.

## Timing against speech: weak evidence

Whisper word times are only good to about 50-100 ms at best, and words arrive every 0.27 s.

- **Words on screen:** the distance from a word's first visible frame to the nearest spoken word start has a median of 50 ms, against 80 ms when the times are shuffled by up to 3 s (n = 890). So words do follow speech, but this can't say more than "within a few frames".
- **Scene cuts:** distance to the nearest word start is 80 ms, against 73 ms when shuffled (n = 381). **No measurable lock of cuts to word starts at this precision.** I can't say whether cuts land on the word or just near it. In the frame strips, new scenes often open empty for about 6 frames and then the word lands, see `motion.md`.
